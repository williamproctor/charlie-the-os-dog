GrowthX · Charlie Franchise · Style Decision

The clay lane is locked.

Charlie is now a fully fictional English bulldog — no real-dog reference behind him — so the art style has to carry identity, ownability, and the entire anti-slop defense on its own. We tested the finalists head-to-head and put the evidence in front of a five-lens judge panel. The verdict was a sweep: claymation for everything, daily. This page is the decision record.

00 · The call

Verdict

Primary lane — everything daily

Claymation (style 03) for all output: video shorts, reaction stills, LinkedIn strips, reply-game ammunition. It swept all five judging lenses (9/10 on each) with no disqualifying flag — the only style that survives both existential tests (anti-slop craft and distinctive-asset ownability) while also winning the daily-utility tests (thumbnail legibility, deadpan register, production consistency).

Everything else

Photoreal (01): retired. Eliminated, not outscored — it rendered three different dogs across three test images, dies at thumbnail size, and at feed size it is functionally Gong's Bruno (a real bulldog, adjacent category): an active misattribution liability with no answer to "why not film a real dog?" Felt (06): no role — everything it does, clay does better, and it breaks the never-cute guardrail. Comic (05): one gated static-editorial role (blog headers, deck art) — never video, never daily.

Why the generic-bulldog decision forced this

The old two-lane system (clay hero / photoreal reactive) was built on a real dog existing: photoreal meant "a real dog reacting," and monthly real-Charlie photos were the anti-slop anchor. With a fictional bulldog, photoreal inverts into the highest-risk lane — a photorealistic genAI dog passing as real is exactly what a slop-literate marketer audience punishes — while clay's handmade-look artifacts read as intentional craft. One locked style also maximizes fluent-device compounding: every post re-encodes one memory structure instead of splitting across two.

01 · How we decided

Method

Ten style samples reviewed; six eliminated on documented grounds; four finalists tested head-to-head with matched renders — same prompts, style swapped — then judged by five independent evaluators, each restricted to a single lens, plus a synthesis chair.

  • Matched test set (14 renders): the reply-game unit ("the stare," 1:1), a two-generation drift pair (same prompt rendered twice — identity consistency under repetition), and a complex 9:16 video keyframe (dusk office, chart on monitor) for each of clay / photoreal / felt; stare + LinkedIn panel for comic.
  • Small-size legibility sheet: each style's stare at 320 / 96 / 48 px — the sizes a reaction still actually lives at in reply threads.
  • Five lenses: feed legibility · anti-slop craft signal · distinctive-asset ownability · comedic register fit · production viability. The two existential lenses (slop, ownability) weighted first per the strategy analysis.

05 · Steelman

The dissent, on the record

The strongest argument against clay

By mid-2026, "Aardman-style claymation" is itself a one-prompt genAI trope. A slop-literate marketer may read the clay coat as costume rather than craft — "AI cosplaying stop-motion" — collapsing the very defense it was chosen for, while courting style-attribution to Wallace & Gromit instead of the brand (fame-without-linkage, the #2 kill risk).

Why it loses: the objection attacks clay's ceiling, not its floor. The alibi holds if — and only if — continuity is enforced: the same pin, the same sweater, the same sets, the same sculpt in every post. A one-prompt trope is a style; a character with locked recurring codes across months is a property. That is executable discipline (below). Photoreal's problem, by contrast, is structural and has no mitigation. The dissent survives as four standing risks:

  • Style-attribution drift ("the Gromit dog") → hard-code proprietary assets; the crest pin is the #1 lock (it varied across test renders — now fixed in the codes below).
  • Garbled pseudo-text props (diplomas, notepads, a hallucinated "CHIEF INSPECTOR B. M. BULLDOG" nameplate) are instant genAI tells → in-scene text is now banned unless explicitly specified; wall art must be abstract. Baked into the render pipeline's negative prompt.
  • i2v character drift at daily volume → seeded reference set, per-asset identity QC, the 10-clip pilot before anything ships.
  • Register drift to twee — the clay medium invites cute → eyes stay half-lidded, no wide-eye, no head-tilt. Now in the negative prompt and the bible.
06 · Character bible v1 — visual spec

The locked Charlie codes

A fictional character's identity is nothing but its codes. These are now hard-coded into every render prompt (preproduce_charlie.py) and are the fluent-device assets every post must re-encode. Changing any of them is a brand decision, not a creative choice.

MEDIUM    stop-motion clay puppet · visible thumbprint texture · hand-built
          miniature sets · practical in-set lighting · painted backdrops
HEAD      tan with a white blaze down the muzzle · graying jowls ·
          heavy sculpted brow ridges (the expression engine)
EYES      HEAVY-LIDDED, always. flat graphic whites, black-dot pupils.
          never wide open. never glossy. the half-closed eye IS the brand.
WARDROBE  navy chunky cable-knit sweater · white shirt collar · dark tie ·
          ONE small gold shield-crest lapel pin (never varies)
WORLD     his office desk · conference room · break room · commute · webcam.
          indoor, office light or screen glow. never a park. never a beach.
TEXT      no readable in-scene text unless the joke requires it — then the
          exact string is specified and must render legibly, or the prop
          text is added post-hoc as an authored overlay.
POSE BAN  wide eyes · head tilts · tongue out · play bows · standing on
          hind legs · anything a plush toy would do
07 · Applied

What changed downstream

  • Supersedes the two-lane call in the one-pager (§04): the reactive lane is now clay too. The speed argument for photoreal is void — the pre-rendered clay reaction library serves the 24–72h window just as fast.
  • The pre-production pack is re-rendered in the clay canon: reference scenes, the 8-still reaction pack, the stack-audit strip, and storyboards SB-05…08 (SB-01…04 and strip 2 were already clay). Retired photoreal originals archived at assets/prepro/_retired-photoreal/. The PDF carousel is rebuilt from clay stills. See the pre-production pack.
  • The anti-slop defense is restated (the "real dog anchor" no longer exists): own the AI-ness in the bio, visible handcraft aesthetic + low cadence as the craft signal, enforced code continuity as the authorship proof, and openly-fictional character lore. "Charlie is real" copy is retired — the standing line becomes: "Charlie is clay. The opinions are real."
  • Bruno separation is now structural: a clay puppet in a tie cannot be mistaken for Gong's photographed bulldog. The quarterly fluency survey stays as the check.
  • The 10-clip i2v pilot runs on clay keyframes (SB-05 first) — the last gate before anything ships publicly.
